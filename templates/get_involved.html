How to get involved in local politics

Local governments make many of their decisions at public meetings, and there are more ways to take part than voting. Below are ten practical ways to get involved, the steps for each, and the official or nonpartisan sources behind them. This page is nonpartisan: it does not tell you which causes, candidates or parties to support.

Rules vary. Open-meetings laws, filing deadlines, application processes and eligibility differ by state, city, county and school district. Use this page to learn what to ask, then confirm details with your clerk or election office. It is general information, not legal advice. Last reviewed {{ gi_checked }}.

Official places to start

  • USAGov: state and local election offices, for help with registering, voting and election results; the office to ask about local filing and sign-up rules.
  • Vote.gov, the federal starting point for registering to vote in your state.
  • USAGov: local governments by state and find and contact elected officials.
  • VOTE411.org from the League of Women Voters Education Fund, for nonpartisan election information.
  • Ballotpedia: state election agencies, with links to state and local election offices.
  • Open Government Guide to your state’s open meetings and open records laws.
